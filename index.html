01 - The ThresholdПраг

Stone, snow, and one door left standing open.

A Serbian church in Ontario begins the way it began in Ras: a low stone wall, a gate that is never locked during a service, and a single lamp burning over the west door so that anyone arriving late can still find it in the dark.

Cross the threshold and the building stops being architecture. The narthex is deliberately cold and dim - it is the world you are leaving. Ahead, the nave is warm with beeswax, and above it the dome carries the one thing every Orthodox church says before a word is spoken: that the roof over the faithful is the sky.

03a - The Parish PriestОтац Ђурађ
Father Đurađ Kojić Parish Priest

Serves the Divine Liturgy, leads the parastos and the Zadušnice, and visits families at home and in hospital. For a baptism, a wedding, a slava or a memorial, Father Đurađ is the first person a family speaks to.

Official parish site 49 N Lake Road, Richmond Hill

Ask him what a family actually asks.

Nobody arrives at a parish office knowing the order of things. These are the questions that come first, and the answers the office gives.

The parish office Answering for Father Đurađ. These are not his own words.

Ask in your own words, or choose one of the questions below. If the office cannot answer it here, it will say so and tell you who can.

Face to face

The twenty minutes are with Father Đurađ Kojić himself, the priest of this parish, and not with the office that answers above.

Twenty minutes with Father Đurađ, on Google Meet.

Some things should not be answered by a page. A death in the family, a marriage, a child not baptised, a question a person has carried for years: for those the parish opens twenty minutes with the priest himself, by video, wherever in the world you are.

  • It is a conversation, not a service. A confession is made in church, in person. This is the talk that comes before it.
  • Serbian or English. Whichever the family is more at home in.
  • Nothing is recorded. What is said stays between you and the priest.
  • If the hour does not suit, write to the office and another will be found. No one is turned away over a time or over ten dollars.

A real candle, a name read aloud, and a photograph that it burned.

A candle on a screen is an intention. This is a wax candle, on the stand in Richmond Hill, lit by a parishioner at the next Liturgy, photographed where it stands, and the picture sent to you. The name written on the slip goes onto the commemoration list and is read at the proskomedia, whether or not you ask for the photograph. If you are in Belgrade, Vancouver or Melbourne, this is the closest you can come.

04 - The Memorial Powered by Saylavy Спомен-зид

Those we carry to the Holy Liturgy.

One wall of the parish hall, and a name on each arch that the parish reads aloud. Behind each one is a Memory Page a family made and Saylavy keeps: a life written down, a recorded voice, photographs, and where they have chosen it, a time capsule that opens on a set day.

The wall belongs to the parish Свети арханђел Гаврило Richmond Hill, Ontario. The parish keeps the remembrance: the arch on this wall, the candles, and the name on the list read at Zadušnice and the Parastos. The pages themselves are kept by Saylavy, and the family keeps ownership.
What a page holds
Memory Pages

A permanent tribute for one person: photographs, recordings, voice and a written life, in one place that does not vanish when a subscription lapses or a platform closes.

Kept, not rented

Bought once. No monthly subscription to lapse, and no renewal somebody has to remember in twenty years, when the people who opened the page are themselves gone.

Proof of Life

A page stays private while a person answers a quiet check-in. When they stop answering, the people they chose are notified. Quiet by design, active when it matters.

Time Capsules

A message or recording that opens on a chosen date: an eighteenth birthday, a wedding, an anniversary. It stays sealed until the day comes.

This is a design demonstration. All six people below are fictional. The names, dates and life stories were written only to show how a Memory Page looks. No real parishioner is represented here.

Add a Memory Page

A page is opened for one person, once. There is nothing technical asked of a family, and nothing has to be ready before you begin: the parish and Saylavy gather it with you, and an unfinished page is still a page.

Open a Memory Page
  1. Tell the parish officeOr tick the box on a parastos request. A telephone call is enough, and the office will not ask you for files.
  2. Gather what there isPhotographs, letters, a recording, what people remember. The parish helps older families with this, and the Serbian school children record the oldest parishioners for the archive.
  3. The page is opened on SaylavyBought once, not rented. The family decides what is public, what stays within the family, and who is notified.
  4. The arch goes up on this wallAnd the name joins the list read at Zadušnice and the Parastos, which the parish does whether or not there is a page.

The parish holds the remembrance, not the data. A family decides what is public and what stays within the family, and may export or close a page at any time. Parish families may submit a Memory Page through the church office or directly with Saylavy: the parish keeps the archive, the family keeps ownership.

Chapter 05 - Дођите и видите

Дођите и видите

The Liturgy is served on Sunday morning and the feast days are kept as they fall. The Serbian school teaches the azbuka and the hymns, the Circle of Serbian Sisters keeps the kitchen, and four Saturdays a year the whole parish prays for its departed at once. Nobody has to know the order of things before they come: the first time, standing at the back is enough.

Come and see. John 1:46
Back to the gate

A parish runs on the people who arrive first and leave last, on a kitchen that never closes, and on a door somebody remembered to unlock.
